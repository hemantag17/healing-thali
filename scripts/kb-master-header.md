# Postpartum Diet & Recovery Knowledge Base — C-Section (Indian Context)

**Version 1.0 | Reviewed 28 October 2026 | English (Indian context)**
**Audience:** An Indian mother recovering from a Cesarean section (and her family), with or without breastfeeding.
**Evidence basis:** ICMR-NIN 2020 Recommended Dietary Allowances & My Plate; WHO maternal postpartum recommendations; ERAS Society cesarean-delivery guidelines; ESPEN surgical nutrition; Academy of Breastfeeding Medicine protocols; IARC monographs; NFHS-5 national data. Numbered references [R1]–[R21] appear at the end of this document. No supplement-industry or commercial-diet-programme content is used anywhere in this knowledge base.

**How this document is organised:**
- **Part 1 — The Nutritional Framework:** energy, macronutrients, micronutrients, hydration, master food lists, supplements.
- **Part 2 — The Timeline-Based Diet Plan:** Day 1–3 (immediate post-op), Day 4–7 (early healing), Day 8–30 (first month), Day 31–60 (second month), Day 61–90 (third month), Day 90+ (long term).
- **Part 3 — Indian Cultural Myths & Fads:** ten traditions examined with scientific verdicts, plus a list of traditions genuinely worth keeping, and a note for the family.
- **Part 4a — 15 Q&A Pairs:** high-frequency questions answered in the assistant's voice.
- **Evidence & References:** [R1]–[R21].

**Standing rules for any assistant using this knowledge base:**
1. Answer only from this document; if something is not covered, say so and direct to the treating obstetrician/paediatrician.
2. Always factor in: days since surgery, breastfeeding status, vegetarian/non-vegetarian, and any complications (anaemia, gestational diabetes, pre-eclampsia, wound issues) — complications mean the treating doctor's instructions override anything here.
3. Medical red flags (fever ≥38°C, wound discharge or gap, bleeding soaking a pad in an hour, severe headache with vision changes, chest pain or breathlessness, calf pain/swelling, no flatus by day 3, thoughts of self-harm) always override dietary conversation — direct to the doctor/hospital immediately (India: 108 ambulance; mental health: Tele-MANAS 14416, Kiran 1800-599-0019).
4. Never validate an unsafe traditional practice to preserve family harmony; correct it with warmth and evidence, and offer the smarter version of the tradition wherever one exists.
